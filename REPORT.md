# Quantum Traffic Gridlock: QUBO and QAOA for two coordinated intersections

Arif, Rohit, Mehul — IBM Qiskit Fall Fest 2026, QC@IITI. Code, outputs and logs: this repository (see `README.md`).
All numbers below come from files in `outputs/` and `logs/` (generated by script).

## 1. Model

Two intersections A and B, four 30-second slots, eight bits x = (A1..A4, B1..B4), 1 = North–South (NS) green, 0 = East–West (EW) green. Per slot and approach: add arrivals (plus, for B's linked approach, vehicles transferred from A); the green approach serves up to `capacity`; the remaining queue q is recorded; `floor(fraction·departures + 0.5)` of A's linked departures join B's linked approach next slot (transfers after slot 4 leave the model). Cost = Σ q (delay) + w_o · Σ max(0, q − storage) (overflow) + w_s · (number of phase switches at each intersection between adjacent slots). All 256 schedules are valid. Our implementation of this recursion agrees with `official/scorer.py` on all 7,936 evaluations (R and 30 random scenarios × 256 schedules; 0 mismatches, `logs/verification.json`). All reported costs come from the scorer.

## 2. QUBO derivation

**Rule (scenario-independent).** The cost is a sum of local terms, one per (intersection, approach, slot): `term = q + w_o·max(0, q − storage)`, where q is the queue after service, plus the switching cost. Causality gives each term a small *causal window*: the queue of an approach at A in slot t depends only on A's bits 1..t; for an unlinked approach at B on B's bits 1..t; for B's linked approach on B's bits 1..t and A's bits 1..t−1 (the vehicles that were transferred). The largest window has 7 bits, so each term is tabulated over at most 128 configurations of its own window; no search over full schedules is involved.

**Quadratic surrogate.** The `min(capacity, queue)` service and the `max(0, ·)` overflow make terms non-quadratic. Each term is replaced by its best quadratic approximation: the degree-2 truncation of its Walsh–Fourier expansion, i.e. the least-squares fit over its window with basis {1, x_i, x_i x_j}. Terms without active nonlinearities are reproduced exactly. The switching cost `w_s(x_t + x_{t−1} − 2x_t x_{t−1})` per adjacent pair is already quadratic and is added exactly. Summing the local fits gives `Q` and an offset over the **8 decision variables; there are no auxiliary variables** (`auxiliary_variables: []`), hence 8 qubits. No penalty terms are needed because every schedule is valid; the overflow weight is part of the cost itself.

**Grounded and optimum-free.** The construction uses only the traffic recursion and scenario parameters; the global optimum is never computed or encoded (enumeration appears only in `baselines.py` as a benchmark). **Verification:** (i) the summed local projections equal the global least-squares quadratic fit of the official cost on all 31 checked scenarios, with maximum absolute difference 1.9e-12; (ii) a test checks that `Q`, `h`, `J` in `qubo.json` give identical energies; (iii) bits outside a term's window never change it (prototype check).

**Surrogate regret** (official cost of the QUBO's exact minimiser versus C\*): on R the minimiser is `11101010`, cost 72.0 = C\*, regret 0 %; QUBO energy and official cost have correlation 0.995. Over 100 random scenarios (section-7 ranges): mean regret 2.06 %, 55 scenarios exactly 0 %, 90 % of scenarios ≤ 5 %, 98 % ≤ 25 %. The worst cases are 32.6 %, 27.0 %, 19.4 %, 17.5 % and 15.3 % (gen004, gen060, gen079, gen098, gen094): overflow nonlinearities there create higher-order interactions a quadratic surrogate cannot represent.

## 3. QAOA design

**Mapping and Ising form.** Schedule bit j sits on qubit 7 − j and is measured into classical bit 7 − j, so Qiskit's printed bitstring reads directly as A1..A4 B1..B4. With x = (1 − z)/2 the QUBO becomes `c + Σ h_i z_i + Σ_{i<j} J_ij z_i z_j` (`to_ising`). We divide energies by σ, the standard deviation under uniformly random bitstrings, which is analytic for an Ising form: σ = √(Σ h_i² + Σ J_ij²). This makes the angle scale comparable across scenarios.

**Circuit.** Hadamards on all qubits, then p = 4 layers of RZ(2γ_k h_i/σ) on each qubit, RZZ(2γ_k J_ij/σ) on each coupled pair, and RX(2β_k) on each qubit; the written `circuit.qasm` (R, seed 0) has 8 H, 96 RZZ (24 coupled pairs × 4 layers), 32 RZ, 32 RX and 8 measurements, depth 46 with RZZ counted as one gate (the QASM defines it as CX–RZ–CX, i.e. 192 CX).

**Initialisation and the sign of β.** Linear ramp γ_k = s_γ (k − ½)/p, β_k = s_β (1 − (k − ½)/p), with (s_γ, s_β) = (1.0, −0.4). The start state is the ground state of −ΣX and the mixer is RX(2β) = exp(−iβX), so an annealing-like path towards the *minimum* of the cost needs β of opposite sign to γ. **We first used (1.0, +0.4), which anneals towards the maximum: the starting normalised energy was +1.4 (random level 0), and COBYLA spent its budget recovering. We found this from `initial_objective` in `metrics.json` and fixed it late on 16 Oct;** the old ramp stays in the ablation table (section 5). Scale choices followed rules fixed before running: the original (0.6, 0.6) → (1.0, +0.4) needed a mean-lift gain ≥ 0.02 on 30 random scenarios (generator seed 2026) without lowering the minimum lift (it gave 0.734 → 0.773), and the sign flip (1.0, +0.4) → (1.0, −0.4) was judged by the same rule (0.773 → 0.820; minimum 0.642 → 0.698; mean initial objective +1.39 → −0.96). Neither R nor H was used for these choices.

**Training.** COBYLA (scipy, `rhobeg` 0.2, `maxiter` 300) minimises the *exact* expectation of the normalised cost Hamiltonian from the statevector (no shots). Every objective call is counted and asserted ≤ 300; the runs use exactly 300 (R, seed 0: objective −0.96 → −1.40). Training is deterministic and does not depend on the seed; **the seed only sets the final sampler** (`StatevectorSampler(seed)`), 4,096 shots. Runtime is 1.28–1.39 s per seed on R (1.27–1.42 s on H) (laptop CPU, noiseless simulation).

## 7. Limitations

- We shipped the wrong-sign ramp until a late review of `initial_objective`; a regression test now requires the starting energy to be below the random level. The ablations were rerun on the corrected ramp; the old-sign rows are kept separately.
- COBYLA uses the whole 300-evaluation budget on every p = 4 run, so the angles are not converged.
- The quadratic surrogate fails on some scenarios: regret up to 32.6 % (10 of 100 scenarios exceed 5 %); on H it costs 2.0 units (0.86 %) and caps the raw best sample at 236.0. Higher-order terms or auxiliary variables were not built.
- The ramp choices were made sequentially on the same 30 scenarios, so part of the gain is likely selection noise; the independent 100-scenario set (0.782 → 0.818) and R (0.739 → 0.781 mean lift) point the same way, but heldout_2 worsened.
- All results are noiseless statevector simulations; no noise model or hardware. Ablations used one sampler seed; mean-lift differences below about 0.02 should not be over-interpreted.
- The hybrid result is reported separately and is not part of the raw counts. We make no quantum-advantage claim: there are only 256 candidate schedules.
