# Quantum Traffic Gridlock: QUBO and QAOA for two coordinated intersections

Arif, Rohit, Mehul — IBM Qiskit Fall Fest 2026, QC@IITI. Code, outputs and logs: this repository (see `README.md`).
All numbers below come from files in `outputs/` and `logs/` (generated by script).

## 1. Model

Two intersections A and B, four 30-second slots, eight bits x = (A1..A4, B1..B4), 1 = North–South (NS) green, 0 = East–West (EW) green. In each slot, for each (intersection, approach): add external arrivals (and, for the linked approach of B, the vehicles transferred from A); the green approach serves up to `capacity` vehicles; the queue q left after service is recorded; on the A→B link, `floor(fraction·departures + 0.5)` vehicles arrive at B's linked approach at the start of the next slot (nothing leaves the model after slot 4). Cost = Σ q (delay) + w_o · Σ max(0, q − storage) (overflow) + w_s · (number of phase switches at each intersection between adjacent slots). All 256 schedules are valid. Our own implementation of this recursion (`traffic_qubo.simulate`) agrees with `official/scorer.py` on every one of 7,936 evaluations (R and 30 random scenarios × 256 schedules; 0 mismatches, `logs/verification.json`). The scorer is the only source of costs in all reported results.

## 2. QUBO derivation

**Rule (scenario-independent).** The cost is a sum of local terms, one per (intersection, approach, slot): `term = q + w_o·max(0, q − storage)`, where q is the queue after service, plus the switching cost. Causality gives each term a small *causal window*: the queue of an approach at A in slot t depends only on A's bits 1..t; for an unlinked approach at B on B's bits 1..t; for B's linked approach on B's bits 1..t and A's bits 1..t−1 (the vehicles that were transferred). The largest window has 7 bits, so each term is tabulated exhaustively over at most 2^7 = 128 configurations of its own window. No search over full schedules is involved.

**Quadratic surrogate.** The `min(capacity, queue)` service and the `max(0, ·)` overflow make terms non-quadratic. Each term is replaced by its best quadratic approximation: the degree-2 truncation of its Walsh–Fourier expansion, i.e. the least-squares fit over its window with basis {1, x_i, x_i x_j}. Terms without active nonlinearities are reproduced exactly. The switching cost `w_s(x_t + x_{t−1} − 2x_t x_{t−1})` per adjacent pair is already quadratic and is added exactly. Summing the local fits gives `Q` and an offset over the **8 decision variables; there are no auxiliary variables** (`auxiliary_variables: []`), hence 8 qubits. No penalty terms are needed because every schedule is valid; the overflow weight is part of the cost itself.

**Why this is grounded and never uses the optimum.** The construction uses only the traffic recursion and the scenario parameters; the same code runs on any scenario, and the global optimum is never computed, searched for or encoded (enumeration appears only in `baselines.py` as a benchmark). **Verification:** (i) the summed local projections equal the global least-squares quadratic fit of the official cost on all 31 checked scenarios, with maximum absolute difference 1.9e-12; (ii) a test confirms that the `Q`, `h`, `J` stored in `qubo.json` give identical energies; (iii) changing bits outside a term's window never changes the term (prototype check).

**Surrogate regret** (official cost of the QUBO's exact minimiser versus C\*): on R the minimiser is `11101010`, cost 72.0 = C\*, regret 0 %; QUBO energy and official cost have correlation 0.995. Over 100 random scenarios (section-7 ranges): mean regret 2.06 %, 55 scenarios exactly 0 %, 90 % of scenarios ≤ 5 %, 98 % ≤ 25 %. The worst cases are 32.6 %, 27.0 %, 19.4 %, 17.5 % and 15.3 % (gen004, gen060, gen079, gen098, gen094): scenarios where overflow nonlinearities create strong higher-order interactions that a quadratic surrogate cannot represent.

## 3. QAOA design

**Mapping and Ising form.** Schedule bit j sits on qubit 7 − j and is measured into classical bit 7 − j, so Qiskit's printed bitstring reads directly as A1..A4 B1..B4. With x = (1 − z)/2 the QUBO becomes `c + Σ h_i z_i + Σ_{i<j} J_ij z_i z_j` (`to_ising`). We divide energies by σ, the standard deviation under uniformly random bitstrings, which is analytic for an Ising form: σ = √(Σ h_i² + Σ J_ij²). This makes the angle scale comparable across scenarios.

**Circuit.** Hadamards on all qubits, then p = 4 layers of RZ(2γ_k h_i/σ) on each qubit, RZZ(2γ_k J_ij/σ) on each coupled pair, and RX(2β_k) on each qubit; the written `circuit.qasm` (R, seed 0) has 8 H, 96 RZZ (24 coupled pairs × 4 layers), 32 RZ, 32 RX and 8 measurements, depth 46 with RZZ counted as one gate (the QASM defines it as CX–RZ–CX, i.e. 192 CX).

**Initialisation.** Linear ramp γ_k = s_γ (k − ½)/p, β_k = s_β (1 − (k − ½)/p). Scales (s_γ, s_β) = (1.0, 0.4) were chosen by a rule fixed *before* running the study: change the original (0.6, 0.6) only if another setting raises the mean lift on 30 random scenarios (generator seed 2026) by ≥ 0.02 without lowering the minimum lift. Of 11 alternatives, two qualified; the best, (1.0, 0.4), raised mean lift 0.734 → 0.773 and minimum lift 0.591 → 0.642. Neither R nor H was used for this choice.

**Training.** COBYLA (scipy, `rhobeg` 0.2, `maxiter` 300) minimises the *exact* expectation of the normalised cost Hamiltonian from the statevector (no shots). Every objective call is counted and asserted ≤ 300; the runs use exactly 300. Training is deterministic and does not depend on the seed; **the seed only sets the final sampler** (`StatevectorSampler(seed)`), 4,096 shots. Runtime is 1.27–1.30 s per seed on R (laptop CPU, noiseless simulation).

## 5. Ablations

Each setting was run on 30 random scenarios (seed 2026) and R (`logs/ablations.md`); lift and enrichment from 4,096 sampled shots (seed 0). Depth and initialisation rows use the original ramp (0.6, 0.6) unless stated.

| setting | mean lift | min lift | mean enrichment | mean regret % | R lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p = 1 | 0.563 | 0.421 | 5.29 | 1.77 | 0.620 |
| p = 2 | 0.665 | 0.524 | 7.33 | 1.77 | 0.688 |
| p = 3 | 0.719 | 0.535 | 8.87 | 1.77 | 0.688 |
| p = 4 (ramp 0.6/0.6) | 0.734 | 0.591 | 9.33 | 1.77 | 0.740 |
| p = 4, ramp 1.0/0.4 (adopted) | 0.773 | 0.642 | 10.74 | 1.77 | 0.740 |
| p = 4, random angles, 3 fixed seeds | 0.622 / 0.741 / 0.686 | 0.096 / 0.547 / 0.305 | 7.75 / 9.72 / 8.23 | 1.77 | 0.663 / 0.738 / 0.729 |
| surrogate degree 1 (linear only) | 0.440 | −0.622 | 4.97 | 41.69 | 0.557 |
| objective CVaR 0.25 (ramp 0.6/0.6) | 0.692 | 0.526 | 10.17 | 1.77 | 0.763 |

Lift grows with depth. The ramp start is more reliable than random angles: all three random starts have a lower minimum lift (0.10–0.55 versus 0.59), and two of the three have a lower mean lift. The quadratic terms are essential: the linear-only surrogate has 41.7 % mean regret and its lift can be negative. CVaR gives higher enrichment but lower mean and minimum lift than the mean-energy objective. There are no free constraint-penalty weights in this model (all schedules are valid; the overflow weight is a scenario parameter), so the surrogate-degree ablation is the corresponding model-side ablation.

## 7. Limitations

- COBYLA uses the whole 300-evaluation budget on every p = 4 run, so the angles are not converged; better optimisation or initialisation might raise lift.
- The quadratic surrogate fails on some scenarios: regret up to 32.6 % (10 of 100 scenarios exceed 5 %). Higher-order terms or auxiliary variables could help but were not built.
- The ramp scales were the best of 11 candidates on 30 scenarios, so part of the +0.04 mean-lift gain is likely selection noise; on R the minimum lift barely moved (0.736 with the old ramp, 0.734 now).
- All results are noiseless statevector simulations; no hardware or noise model was used. Sampling is from the exact output distribution.
- Ablations used one sampler seed (0) and 30 random scenarios; differences below about 0.02 in mean lift should not be over-interpreted.
- The classical hybrid result is reported separately and is not part of the raw QAOA counts. We make no quantum-advantage claim: the problem has 256 candidate schedules.
